Rules the AI can’t break

How a Namesake story is built.

Every story is built to a written specification, shaped to a child of exactly their age: easy enough to follow, surprising enough to be worth hearing twice.

You set the brief.

Choose the theme and the characters, and add the idea itself if they have given you one. A narwhal who is also a firefighter, for instance. And unicorns. Always unicorns.

Nine story shapes.

Every story is built on one of nine named shapes, from a quiet wander to a small quest, with a gentler set kept for two to five year olds.

How much goes wrong is a choice.

Every story needs something to go a little wrong. How much, and how frightening, is set by age, and for the under-threes it is turned right down.

It resolves before it ends.

The ending has to settle the story’s central problem before the final beat. No cliffhangers at bedtime.

Vocabulary and pacing by age.

Sentence length and word choice shift by age band, rather than one reading level for everyone.

Villains stay silly.

If a young child’s story has a villain at all, they are bumbling rather than frightening.

Finished before it plays.

The story is written and finished in advance, never generated live while they listen. Nothing changes once you have approved it.

Screen stays off
