Written to a specification

How a Yonder story is built.

You set the brief, then an AI service crafts the story based on a research-driven model of how children's bedtime stories actually work. Structure, pacing, and language a child that age can follow.

  • You set the brief. Choose the theme and the characters, and add the idea itself if they have given you one. A narwhal who is also a firefighter, for instance. And unicorns. Always unicorns.
  • It already knows them. Their name, age, interests and who lives in their world are saved once. You are not setting it all up again every night.
  • Nine story shapes. Every story is built on one of nine named shapes, from a quiet wander to a small quest, with a gentler set kept for two to five year olds.
  • How much goes wrong is a choice. Every story needs something to go a little wrong. How much, and how frightening, is set by age, and for the under-threes it is turned right down.
  • It resolves before it ends. The ending has to settle the story's central problem before the final beat. No cliffhangers at bedtime.
  • Vocabulary and pacing by age. Sentence length and word choice shift by age band, rather than one reading level for everyone.
  • Villains stay silly. If a young child's story has a villain at all, they are bumbling rather than frightening.
  • Finished before it plays. The story is written and finished in advance, never generated live while they listen. Nothing changes once you have approved it.

Made for parents

You choose what they hear.

A box of story figures plays the same fixed catalogue every night, however many you buy. Yonder adds a new one whenever they want it, through the speaker you already have.

A story on Spotify or YouTube Kids is unvetted and autoplays into whatever comes next. A Yonder story is one you approved, and then it stops.
