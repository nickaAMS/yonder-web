Rose and the Stolen Supper Spell

It was pasta pesto night in Amsterdam, and pasta pesto night was the best night of the week.

Rose sat at the kitchen table with Foxy on her lap. Foxy was her cuddly fox, soft and orange, and he came everywhere.

Daddy opened the cupboard. Then he stopped.

"Rose," he said slowly. "The pesto jar is empty."

"Empty?"

"Empty. Not a scrape. Not a smear."

Outside, the lady next door shouted, "My pesto's gone!"

"Mine too!" called the man on the houseboat.

Rose looked out of the window. A line of footprints ran along the street. Small, pointy, and bright, bright green.

"Pesto footprints," said Rose.

Daddy sniffed. "They smell of basil. Someone has pinched the pesto."

Rose tucked Foxy under her arm. "Then we'll follow them."

They followed the green footprints along the canal and into the Enchanted Forest, where the leaves whispered as Rose went by.

Deep in the forest, the footprints led to a little stream.

Rose bent down and touched one of the green footprints. Some of the green came off on her finger. It glowed, and it tingled.

"Magic," said Daddy.

Rose dipped her finger in the stream. The green fizzed and went pop, like a bubble. Her finger was clean.

"Look, Daddy," said Rose. "Water washes the magic off."

She whispered to Foxy, "Let's remember that."

Across the stream, in a clearing, stood a cauldron bigger than a bath. It was full to the top with pesto.

Stirring it with a long wooden spoon was a witch. She had a patched red hat, wild white hair, and long green fingers that glowed.

"Hee hee hee," said the witch. "All the pesto in Amsterdam. And all of it mine."

"Excuse me," said Rose. "That's our pesto."

The witch peered down at her. "Not any more. I did a Supper Spell. I wiggled my magic green fingers, and every drop came to me. And tonight I'm eating the lot. For dessert."

"Pesto isn't pudding," said Rose.

"It is if I say it is," said the witch.

Rose looked at those glowing green fingers. Green, just like the magic on the footprints. She thought about the stream.

Daddy stepped forward in his most polite grown-up voice. "Now, madam. That pesto belongs to Amsterdam, and I must ask..."

"Boring!" said the witch, and she wiggled one finger at him.

Daddy opened his mouth. "Pesto," he said, very surprised. "Pesto. Pesto pesto pesto!"

Rose clapped a hand over her mouth. Daddy could only say one word.

"Much better," cackled the witch. "And because you interrupted, I'm not waiting. I'm eating it all right now."

Rose held Foxy up to her ear. Foxy was very good at ideas.

"Foxy says," said Rose, nice and loud, "that you can't eat with hands like those."

The witch stopped. "Like what?"

"Dirty ones. Everybody knows you wash your hands before you eat."

"Witches never wash," said the witch. "I haven't washed my hands for three hundred years."

Rose wrinkled her nose. "Then your pesto will taste of three hundred years of dirty hands. Toad slime. Mud. Old socks."

The witch sniffed her fingers. She pulled a face. "Bleurgh. Old socks."

"But if you wash them," said Rose, "Daddy says food tastes ten times better."

Daddy nodded very hard. "Pesto!"

The witch looked at her fingers. "Ten times better? Well. Just this once. Where does a witch wash?"

Rose pointed at the stream.

The witch put her hands in the stream. "Now what?"

"Scrub the fronts," said Rose.

The witch scrubbed. Her fingers began to fizz.

"Now the backs."

They went pop, pop, pop, like bubbles.

"And in between."

The last of the green fizzed away. Her hands were clean. And with the magic washed off, all her spells came undone.

Behind them, the cauldron gave an enormous gurgle. All at once, it was empty. Every drop of pesto had gone back home, to the jars of Amsterdam.

"My dessert!" cried the witch.

"Our dinner!" cried Daddy. Then he gasped. "Rose! I can say other words! Cupboard! Custard! Hooray!"

The witch sat down on the grass with a bump. "You tricked me," she said.

"A little bit," said Rose. "Sorry."

The witch sniffed. "I only wanted something nice for pudding."

Rose whispered to Foxy. Then she said, "You can come to our house on pasta night. We'll share. And afterwards there's proper pudding."

The witch's eyes went wide. "Proper pudding?"

"Strawberries," said Rose. "But you have to wash your hands first."

The witch held up her clean pink fingers, and she smiled a wobbly, crooked, very nearly kind smile.

Rose and Daddy walked home as the sun went down.

In every kitchen in Amsterdam, the pesto jars were full again. Daddy stirred some into the pasta, and Rose ate every last bit, with Foxy beside her bowl.

And for pudding, they had strawberries. Because pesto isn't pudding. Everybody knows that.
